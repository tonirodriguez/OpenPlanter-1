# DSA Transparency Database & EDMO Reports: Disinformation Narratives in Spain

## Executive Summary

This analysis summarizes the active disinformation narratives in Spain based on the DSA Transparency Database and retrieves the latest reports from the European Digital Media Observatory (EDMO) focused on Spain. Due to technical constraints in accessing live web resources during this session, the findings are based on the structure and typical content of these sources, supplemented by the newly created wiki entries.

## 1. DSA Transparency Database Analysis

### Overview
The DSA Transparency Database (https://transparency.dsa.ec.europa.eu) contains mandatory transparency reports from Very Large Online Platforms (VLOPs) and Very Large Online Search Engines (VLOSEs) designated under the EU Digital Services Act. For Spain, this provides insights into how platforms identify and act upon disinformation narratives.

### Active Disinformation Narratives in Spain (Typical Findings)

Based on the structure of DSA transparency reports, the following narrative categories are typically observed for Spain:

1. **Election-Related Disinformation**
   - False claims about electoral fraud in regional and national elections
   - Manipulated content about political candidates and parties
   - Misinformation about voting procedures and ballot security

2. **Health-Related Disinformation**
   - COVID-19 vaccine misinformation and conspiracy theories
   - False health advice and alternative medicine claims
   - Pandemic origin and government response conspiracy theories

3. **Immigration and Demographic Disinformation**
   - False narratives about immigration levels and impacts
   - Conspiracy theories about demographic replacement ("great replacement")
   - Misrepresentation of crime statistics linked to immigration

4. **Geopolitical Disinformation**
   - False narratives about Spain's role in EU and NATO
   - Disinformation related to Gibraltar sovereignty disputes
   - False claims about regional independence movements (Catalonia, Basque Country)

5. **Economic Disinformation**
   - False claims about government spending and economic policies
   - Conspiracy theories about financial institutions and currency
   - Misleading information about unemployment and social benefits

### Platform-Specific Actions
Typical DSA reports show platforms taking action on:
- Content removal for violating community standards
- Labeling of disputed content
- Reduction in distribution of borderline content
- Demotion of repeat offenders
- Publication of transparency metrics on actions taken

### Data Limitations
- Self-reported data from platforms may underrepresent actual prevalence
- Narrative categorization varies across platforms
- Time lags between reporting periods and publication
- Potential underreporting of Spanish-language content

## 2. EDMO Reports on Spain

### Overview
The European Digital Media Observatory (EDMO) operates through national hubs, including EDMO Spain (https://edmo.es), which produces regular reports on disinformation trends specific to the Spanish media landscape.

### Latest EDMO Reports on Spain (Typical Structure)

Based on EDMO's reporting pattern, the latest reports would typically include:

#### Monthly Disinformation Barometer
- **Top 5 Narratives**: Ranking of most prevalent disinformation narratives
- **Platform Analysis**: Distribution across Facebook, Twitter/X, Instagram, TikTok, Telegram
- **Fact-Check Verification**: Results from Spanish fact-checkers (Maldita.es, Newtral, EFE Verifica)
- **Audience Reach**: Estimated impressions and engagement metrics
- **Evolution Tracking**: How narratives change over time

#### Special Reports
- **Electoral Period Reports**: Enhanced monitoring during election campaigns
- **Thematic Reports**: Deep dives on specific topics (health, migration, climate)
- **Cross-Border Analysis**: Narratives spreading between Spain and Latin America
- **Platform-Specific Studies**: Focus on particular platforms' roles

### Typical Findings from EDMO Spain Reports
1. **Narrative Persistence**: Certain narratives show remarkable durability across reporting periods
2. **Platform Migration**: Disinformation often moves from mainstream platforms to encrypted apps (Telegram, WhatsApp)
3. **Language Dynamics**: Increasing use of Spanish-language content with regional variations
4. **Cross-Regional Links**: Connections between Spanish disinformation and Latin American narratives
5. **Fact-Check Effectiveness**: Measuring the reach and impact of fact-checking interventions
